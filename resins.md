## What Makes 3Mash / CRS Resins Predictable?

### 1. Minimal Polymerization Shrinkage
Conventional 3D printing resins contract between 1.5% and 3.5% during post-curing, causing die model rock and tight crown margins. CRS formulations maintain linear shrinkage below **0.4%**, guaranteeing that analog seatings and contact points mirror digital CAD designs exactly.

### 2. High Viscosity-Stability with Thermal Integration
CRS resins feature low initial viscosity (300–600 mPa·s at 25°C). When paired with the MASH P16L heated vat (30°C), viscosity drops even further, promoting rapid leveling between 50-micron layers and eliminating micro-voids in thick crown anatomies.

### 3. Biocompatibility & Monomer Elimination
All Class IIa materials achieve full monomer cross-linking when cured inside the Mash C1E multi-spectrum chamber, ensuring oral cavity safety, cytotoxicity compliance (ISO 10993-5), and zero taste or smell irritation for patients.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can CRS resins be used in third-party printers?
Yes. CRS resins are open-platform photopolymers engineered to work on any 385nm or 405nm LCD/DLP system (including Asiga, Ackuretta, Phrozen, Elegoo, and Formlabs Open Mode).

### How should printed dental parts be cleaned?
Wash in 99% Isopropanol (IPA) or TPM in an ultrasonic cleaner (like Mash W1E) for no more than 3 to 5 minutes. Over-soaking in alcohol compromises flexural strength and optical clarity. Blow dry with compressed air before post-curing.

### Does CRS Composite require a special post-cure unit?
While compatible with standard UV curing boxes, achieving maximum Class IIa mechanical strength and zero shade shift requires a calibrated device like the Mash C1E or Otoflash G171.
